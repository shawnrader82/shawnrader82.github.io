The current consular legalization chain

How Does Thailand Document Legalization Work Right Now?

Because the Apostille Convention does not enter into force for Thailand until February 28, 2027, many U.S. documents still follow the current authentication and consular legalization route. The exact steps depend on the document and receiving authority. We coordinate the U.S.-side steps.

Step 1 — California Secretary of State

  • State-issued documents (birth, marriage, death certificates, court orders) go directly to the California Secretary of State for authentication of the issuing official's seal.
  • A California notary public must first sign and seal powers of attorney, income letters, affidavits, and single-status declarations. The California Secretary of State then authenticates them.
  • Diplomas and academic records from California institutions typically require notarization of a certified copy before California SOS authentication.
  • Typical processing: 3–10 business days depending on submission method.

Step 2 — U.S. Department of State

  • The U.S. Dept of State in Washington D.C. authenticates the California Secretary of State's seal. The Royal Thai Embassy and Thai Consulates generally require this federal authentication step.
  • Standard: approximately 4–8 weeks. Expedited handling, when available: approximately 7–10 business days.
  • After Step 2, your document is ready for the Royal Thai consular legalization step.

Step 3 — Royal Thai Embassy or Consulate (consular legalization)

  • The Royal Thai Embassy in Washington, D.C., or the Royal Thai Consulate-General in Los Angeles applies the final Thai legalization stamp. Thai government authorities then accept the document.
  • Each Thai consular post covers specific U.S. states. For California documents, the Royal Thai Consulate-General in Los Angeles typically has jurisdiction. However, some document types or Thai government requests may specify the Embassy in Washington, D.C. Call us to confirm before submitting.
  • Thai legalization adds approximately 1–2 weeks after the consular office receives your documents, depending on the office and current volume.
  • Many Thai offices require certified Thai translations alongside the legalized original — confirm translation requirements with your Thai attorney or the receiving Thai authority before submitting.

Thailand legalization FAQ

Questions about document legalization for Thailand.

When will Thailand begin accepting apostilles?

Thailand deposited its instrument of accession to the Hague Apostille Convention on June 30, 2026. The Convention enters into force for Thailand on February 28, 2027. Until then, U.S. documents continue through the current authentication and Thai consular legalization process rather than the apostille-only route.

What is the current legalization chain to Thailand?

Until the Apostille Convention enters into force for Thailand on February 28, 2027, many California-issued documents continue through three stages: California Secretary of State authentication, U.S. Department of State authentication, and legalization by the appropriate Royal Thai diplomatic post. The exact route depends on the document and receiving authority. We coordinate the U.S.-side steps.

Royal Thai Consulate-General Los Angeles vs. Royal Thai Embassy Washington D.C. — which do I use?

Each Royal Thai consular post in the U.S. serves a specific jurisdiction. For California clients, the Royal Thai Consulate-General in Los Angeles typically handles California-issued documents. However, some document types or specific Thai government requests may direct you to the Royal Thai Embassy in Washington D.C. Sending documents to the wrong office can cause significant delays. Call us before your order so we can confirm the correct consular office for your specific document and its intended use in Thailand.

Do I need a certified Thai translation?

Many Thai government offices require certified Thai translations of English-language U.S. documents. This commonly applies to marriage registration, visa applications, and court or property matters. Even if the Royal Thai Embassy accepts English documents for legalization, the receiving Thai authority often requires a Thai translation. Confirm the requirements with the specific Thai office or your Thai attorney before submitting.

How long does Royal Thai legalization take?

California processing commonly takes 3–10 business days. U.S. Department of State processing is approximately 4–8 weeks standard or 7–10 business days with available expedited handling. Royal Thai Embassy or Consulate-General processing can add approximately 1–2 weeks after receipt. Timing varies by document, office, and current volume.

When will Thailand officially accept apostilles?

Thailand deposited its instrument of accession with the HCCH on June 30, 2026. The Hague Apostille Convention enters into force for Thailand on February 28, 2027. Until that date, the current authentication and Thai consular legalization route remains applicable.

Will documents legalized now still be valid after Thailand joins the Hague Convention?

Thailand's transition to the apostille process does not automatically invalidate documents completed under the legalization procedure applicable at the time. Final acceptance always depends on the receiving Thai authority and its date, document, and translation requirements. If your submission will occur after February 28, 2027, confirm the required route before ordering.
