Why Washington DC

Federal documents can only be apostilled in Washington DC.

Under the Hague Apostille Convention, federal U.S. records — documents issued by a U.S. federal agency — are apostilled exclusively by the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications at 600 19th Street NW, Washington DC 20006. The California Secretary of State cannot apostille an FBI background check, a USCIS Naturalization Certificate, or a federal court order. This is the same authority for every state in the country. Anyone in Los Angeles who needs a federal Apostille has three practical options: mail the documents to Washington DC themselves, hire a service that mails documents to DC on their behalf, or hire a service (like ours) that coordinates with a DC-based hand-delivery partner for faster turnaround.

What "Washington DC service" actually means

Straight talk about how LA-based Apostille services work.

Every California Apostille service that advertises "Washington DC federal Apostille" is either mailing documents from California to the State Department (the same option you have on your own) or working with a courier or partner in DC. Neither one has State Department staff. We tell you exactly which happens on your job: for hand-delivery tier, our DC partner physically walks your documents into the Office of Authentications; for mail-in tier, we prepare the package and mail it from Los Angeles. Both are legitimate services. The difference is turnaround (~9 business days versus 5–8 weeks) and cost. If someone tells you they can walk in and get a federal Apostille the same day without a documented emergency, they are misinformed — the State Department's same-day appointments are strictly limited to life-or-death, 48-hour travel, federal agency business, or deportation.

Good to know

  • Federal ≠ California: a California-issued birth certificate does not go to Washington DC. Only federally-issued records do.
  • Recency matters: FBI reports and some IRS letters need to be issued within 3–6 months of foreign submission.
  • Original signatures: most federal documents must be originals with wet-ink signatures or the FBI's embedded digital signature — photocopies are rejected.
  • Translation: the Apostille itself does not translate the document. Certified translation, if required by the destination, happens after Apostille.

Washington DC federal Apostille FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Why does a federal document have to be apostilled in Washington DC?

Under the Hague Apostille Convention, federal U.S. documents (FBI reports, USCIS certificates, NARA records, federal court documents, and U.S. Department of State-issued documents) are apostilled only by the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications at 600 19th Street NW, Washington DC. State Secretaries of State (including California) cannot apostille federal records.

How much does the U.S. Department of State charge for a federal Apostille?

The U.S. Department of State charges $20 per document, payable by check or money order. This fee is set by federal law, is non-refundable even if the document is rejected, and applies to every federal document regardless of type. Our service fee is separate and covers preparation of Form DS-4194, coordination with our DC hand-delivery partner, tracking, and secure return shipping.

How fast can you get a federal Apostille?

Standard mail-in through the U.S. Department of State takes about 5 weeks from receipt. Our DC hand-delivery partner drops documents at the Office of Authentications' walk-in counter (Monday through Thursday, 7:30 to 9:00 AM) for pickup approximately 9 business days later. Same-day appointments at the State Department are available only in narrow circumstances: documented life-or-death emergencies, verified travel within 48 hours, U.S. federal agency official business, or active deportation proceedings.

Do you actually have staff in Washington DC?

We are a Los Angeles-based Apostille service. We work with a trusted DC-based partner who hand-delivers our clients' documents to the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications and picks them up on the pickup date. This is common industry practice — most California Apostille services either mail documents to DC themselves or coordinate with a DC courier or partner. We are transparent about which step happens where.

Which federal documents get apostilled at the U.S. Department of State?

The most common are FBI Identity History Summary (background check) reports, USCIS Naturalization Certificates and Certificates of Citizenship, NARA (National Archives) records, federal court documents, Consular Reports of Birth Abroad (CRBA/FS-240) and Death Abroad, U.S. Department of State-issued documents, and federally-issued Social Security and IRS letters where the destination country requires federal-level authentication.

Can California-issued documents be apostilled in Washington DC?

No. California-issued documents (California birth, marriage, death, divorce certificates, notarized documents signed by a California Notary, California court records) are apostilled by the California Secretary of State, not the U.S. Department of State in Washington DC. We handle California state Apostilles in Los Angeles with same-day service available. Only federally-issued records go to Washington DC.

Do I need consular legalization in addition to the Washington DC Apostille?

Only if your destination country is not a member of the Hague Apostille Convention. For Hague countries, the U.S. Department of State Apostille is sufficient. For non-Hague countries (some Gulf States, several Southeast Asian and African nations, and others), the document also needs consular legalization at the destination country's embassy in Washington DC. We coordinate the full chain when required.

What is Form DS-4194 and do I need to fill it out myself?

Form DS-4194 is the U.S. Department of State's Request for Authentications Service form. It must accompany every federal document submitted for Apostille. We prepare it for you as part of our service — you don't need to fill it out. Errors on DS-4194 are one of the most common reasons federal Apostille requests get rejected and delayed by weeks.
