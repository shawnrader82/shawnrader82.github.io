Common Ventura County Apostille orders

Which Ventura County documents get apostilled.

Vital records

California birth, marriage, death, and divorce certificates issued by Ventura County County Recorder or CDPH Vital Records. Certified copies with a raised seal are required — photocopies are not eligible.

Notarized documents

Powers of attorney, affidavits, consent letters, statutory declarations, and any other document signed in front of a California Notary Public. As a mobile Notary service, we notarize on-site anywhere in Ventura County and route the document straight to Apostille.

Corporate documents

California corporate resolutions, articles of incorporation, certificates of good standing, and officer certifications for foreign subsidiaries, joint ventures, and international contracting.

School records

Diplomas, degrees, and transcripts from California high schools, colleges, and universities. Registrar signatures typically need California Notary verification before Apostille — we coordinate the full chain.

Court records

California court judgments, divorce decrees, and certified court records with a Court Clerk certification. Adoption records, name changes, and judgment liens are common types.

Naval Base Ventura County documents

Active-duty and retired military at Naval Base Ventura County frequently need Apostilled powers of attorney, service records, and family documents for overseas assignments and dependents abroad.

Agricultural & export documents

Oxnard, Camarillo, and the Santa Clara River Valley are agricultural exporters. Certificates of origin and USDA-adjacent documents sometimes require California Apostille.

Cross-border families

Ventura County has a large population with family ties to Mexico, the Philippines, and Central America. Birth, marriage, and school records for family use abroad are common Apostille orders.

Ventura County Apostille FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Do I have to drive to Sacramento to get an Apostille from Ventura County?

No. The California Secretary of State has two Apostille walk-in counters — Sacramento and Los Angeles (300 South Spring Street). Every county in Southern California, including Ventura County, uses the LA counter for same-day service. We coordinate LA counter service for Ventura County clients so you don't need to drive it yourself.

How much does a California Apostille cost?

The California Secretary of State charges $20 per document, plus a $6 special-handling fee per document for in-person walk-in submissions. Our service fee is separate and covers pickup, notarization if needed, LA counter service, tracking, and secure return delivery. Call or text with your document type and destination country for an exact quote.

How long does a California Apostille take?

Walk-in at the California SoS Los Angeles office is same-day for qualifying documents received before 4:30 PM — typically processed within 30 minutes at the counter. Mail-in through the Sacramento office is currently running about 5 weeks. We recommend LA walk-in for anyone with a real deadline.

What documents from Ventura County can be apostilled?

Any California-issued document with a proper California official's signature. That includes California birth, marriage, death, and divorce certificates issued by California county recorders or CDPH Vital Records; documents notarized by a California Notary Public; California court documents certified by the clerk; California corporate documents from the California Secretary of State Business Programs Division; and California school transcripts and diplomas. Federal documents (FBI reports, USCIS certificates, NARA records) are apostilled separately by the U.S. Department of State in Washington DC, not the California SoS.

Can you pick up my documents from my home or office in Ventura County?

Yes. As a mobile Notary service, we come to you throughout Ventura County. We can notarize any documents that need a Notary first, pick up already-notarized documents or certified vital records, and handle the entire Apostille process from your address to your final delivery address.

Do I need consular legalization in addition to the California Apostille?

Only if your destination country is not a member of the Hague Apostille Convention. For Hague countries (most of Europe, Latin America, Japan, South Korea, and dozens more), the Apostille alone is sufficient. For non-Hague countries (some Gulf States, several Southeast Asian and African nations), the document also needs consular legalization at the destination country's consulate — typically in Los Angeles or San Francisco. We coordinate the full chain when required.

Can I combine my Apostille order with Notary or translation services from Ventura County?

Yes. We routinely combine mobile Notary, certified translation, and Apostille into a single order for Ventura County clients. That's usually the fastest path — one team handles notarization, certified translation (24-hour standard for up to 10 pages; up to 72 hours for larger jobs), and Apostille certification, so nothing falls through the cracks between vendors.
