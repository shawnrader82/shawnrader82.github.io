About Santa Ana City Jail

Jail notary service at Santa Ana City Jail, Civic Center Plaza.

Santa Ana City Jail, at 62 Civic Center Plaza inside the Santa Ana Police Department's main station, is a municipal ‘Type I’ jail — one of the few full-service city jails in California, meaning it can hold people longer than a typical 24-96 hour city booking facility. It is a completely separate system from the Orange County Sheriff's Central Jail Complex (CJX) on North Flower Street: different booking records, different inmate lookup, different visiting procedures. Santa Ana City Jail has a documented history of housing federal detainees under contract — it held U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detainees from 2006 until the city ended that contract in 2017, and separately has held U.S. Marshals Service federal prisoners and material witnesses under an intergovernmental service agreement. Families and attorneys call us when a signature can’t wait for release, regardless of which system holds the signer.

Mobile Notary FAQ — Santa Ana City Jail

Frequently asked questions.

Is Santa Ana City Jail the same as the OC Sheriff's jail?

No. Santa Ana City Jail is a municipal jail operated by the Santa Ana Police Department at 62 Civic Center Plaza. It is an entirely separate system from the Orange County Sheriff's Department's Central Jail Complex (CJX) on North Flower Street, a few blocks away. They keep separate booking records, separate inmate lookup systems, and separate visiting/professional-visit procedures. Confirm which system someone is in before you assume.

Is Santa Ana City Jail a federal jail?

Not currently. Santa Ana City Jail is a city-run municipal facility, one of the few full-service ‘Type I’ city jails in California, meaning it can hold inmates longer than a typical 24–96 hour city booking facility. It has a documented history with federal agencies — it housed ICE immigration detainees from 2006 until the city ended that contract in 2017, and separately has held U.S. Marshals Service federal pretrial detainees and material witnesses under an intergovernmental service agreement. As of 2025 the City of Santa Ana has stated it holds no ICE contract. If you were told the person is in ‘federal custody’ at this address, we recommend confirming directly with Santa Ana PD, since the facility can hold city, state, and federal-hold inmates depending on current agreements.

Where is Santa Ana City Jail located?

62 Civic Center Plaza, Santa Ana, CA 92701, at the corner of Boyd Way and 6th Street, near Civic Center Drive inside the Santa Ana Police Department's main station. Public parking is available in the structure across the street; there is a fee and no validation is provided.

How do I confirm someone is in custody at Santa Ana City Jail?

There is no public online inmate locator for Santa Ana City Jail — it is a separate system from the OC Sheriff's ‘Who's In Jail’ portal, which only covers county facilities (Central Jail Complex, Theo Lacy, and James A. Musick). Call the Santa Ana Police Department directly at (714) 245-8100 (jail line) or (714) 245-8665 (front desk/records) with the inmate's full legal name and date of birth.

What are the visiting hours at Santa Ana City Jail?

The jail front lobby is open daily 7:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. Inmate visitation is Tuesday through Friday at 10 a.m., 2 p.m., 4 p.m., 7 p.m., and 9 p.m., and Saturday/Sunday at 8 a.m., 10 a.m., 2 p.m., 4 p.m., 7 p.m., and 9 p.m. The lobby is closed 11:30 a.m.–1 p.m. and 9 p.m.–7 a.m. Professional visits for attorneys and notaries typically have more flexibility than these public visiting blocks — we confirm current professional-visit access with the facility before scheduling.

What ID does the inmate need for notarization at Santa Ana City Jail?

No personal photo ID is required. Santa Ana City Jail is operated by the Santa Ana Police Department, so identification there is issued by the police department rather than by a county sheriff. The inmate wears an SAPD booking wristband matched to the booking record. All we need from you is the inmate's full legal name and booking number.

How long does a notary appointment at Santa Ana City Jail take?

Plan for 1 to 3 hours total including travel from Los Angeles, security screening, wait time, and the signing itself. The notarization is quick once we're in the professional visiting area; most of the time is travel and facility processing.
