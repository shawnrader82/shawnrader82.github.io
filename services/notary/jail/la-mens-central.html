About Men’s Central Jail and IRC

Notary information for Men’s Central Jail and the Inmate Reception Center.

Mobile Notary FAQ — Men’s Central Jail and IRC

Frequently asked questions.

Can a document be notarized for someone at Men’s Central Jail or the Inmate Reception Center?

It may be possible. LASD policy recognizes notary visits for inmates who need legal documents notarized, but facility access, inmate availability, and current procedures must be confirmed before a visit can be arranged. Contact us with the inmate’s full legal name, booking number if available, document type, and deadline. Service is not guaranteed.

Is the Inmate Reception Center the same as Men’s Central Jail?

No. Men’s Central Jail and the Inmate Reception Center are separate facilities in the downtown Los Angeles custody complex on Bauchet Street. IRC handles booking, processing, classification, transfers, and releases. We confirm the reported location before discussing whether a notary visit may be possible.

What information do you need before I ask about a jail notary visit?

Please provide the inmate’s full legal name, booking number if available, reported facility, document type, your contact information, and any deadline. You can search the LASD Inmate Information Center using the person’s name and date of birth, or call LASD Inmate Information at (213) 473-6100.

Can you notarize documents for someone who was just booked or is still being processed?

It may be possible, but timing can be difficult. A person in intake or processing may be transferred, released, in court, receiving medical care, or otherwise unavailable before a visit can be arranged. For an urgent matter, contact us with the document type and deadline so we can discuss the current situation. If the person is moved, service may need to be reconsidered for the new facility.

How do I get documents to the notary?

Please send or provide the complete document before any visit is scheduled. Professional notary visits are separate from regular family visitation, and facility rules can limit what may be brought inside. Documents must be complete and ready to sign; a notary cannot draft, fill out, choose, or give legal advice about a document.

What identification does an inmate need for notarization?

California law may permit a notary to rely on sheriff-issued inmate identification for a signer in a local detention facility when it provides satisfactory evidence of identity. The notary evaluates the identification available through facility procedures at the time of notarization. Please provide the inmate’s full legal name and booking number when you contact us.

What if the inmate has been transferred or released?

Transfers and releases can change the available options. Let us know the new location or expected release date, and we can discuss whether service may be possible at another facility or after release. A new location may affect timing, availability, and travel fees.

What if the inmate refuses or cannot sign?

A notary cannot force a person to sign. If the inmate refuses to sign, appears unwilling, or cannot communicate a willingness to sign, the notarization cannot be completed. Depending on the circumstances and travel already performed, a trip fee may still apply.
