Limits worth knowing before you order

  • You buy the whole document. Recorders will not sell a single page or an excerpt, so the final cost tracks the document’s full page count.
  • Page counts are unknown until we’re at the counter. We ask you to authorize a not-to-exceed amount for county fees in writing before the trip.
  • Search fees are non-refundable. If the index search turns up nothing, LA County still keeps the fee.
  • Pre-1985 LA records are not same-day. They come from off-site archives and usually add about two business days.
  • Beverly Hills cannot certify. Certified copies have to be issued from Norwalk, Van Nuys, or Lancaster in LA County.
  • Vital records are a different service. Birth, death, and marriage certificates are restricted by law and cannot be purchased on your behalf without a sworn statement you sign yourself.

Common questions

Records Retrieval FAQ.

How much does a certified copy of a recorded document cost in Los Angeles County?

Los Angeles County charges $6.00 for the first page and $3.00 for each additional page of a certified copy. Plain copies are $5.00 for the first page and $3.00 for each additional page. A three-page certified grant deed is therefore $12.00 in county fees. There is no flat per-document copy fee — the price scales with page count.

How much do certified copies cost in Orange and Ventura County?

Orange County charges $1.00 per page plus $1.00 to certify each document, so a three-page certified copy is $4.00. Ventura County charges $2.00 for the first page, $1.00 for each additional page, and $1.00 for certification, so a three-page certified copy is $5.00.

What information do you need before you can retrieve a document?

Identifiers the recorder can actually search on: the document number, or the book and page, or the grantor and grantee names plus the approximate year of recording. Recorder indexes are organized by name and document number.

Can you look up a recorded document using only the property address?

No. The official records index is a grantor/grantee name and document number index and cannot be searched by street address. If an address is all you have, the owner names need to be identified first — then we can search by name and year.

Do I need to authorize you or prove ownership to buy a recorded deed?

No. Recorded real estate documents — deeds, deeds of trust, liens, reconveyances — are public records in California. Anyone may purchase a copy and no identification, authorization, or proof of ownership is required. Vital records are the opposite: birth, death, and marriage certificates are restricted by law and require a sworn statement signed by an authorized requester.

How fast can you get the document?

In LA County, records from 1985 forward are generally produced same-day at the counter, so same-business-day delivery is often possible. Records older than 1985 are pulled from off-site archives and typically add about two business days. Mail and online copy requests to the LA recorder are quoted at roughly 20 working days — walking in is the fast path.

Is there a search fee, and is it refundable?

LA County charges a staff index search fee of $0.50 per name per year with a $1.00 minimum, and it is non-refundable — the county keeps it even if no matching document is located. Orange and Ventura staff do not search for the public, so a document number or book and page is required in those counties.

Can I buy just one page of a recorded document?

No. Recorders issue the complete document only. There is no way to purchase a single page or an excerpt, which is why the final cost depends on the document’s total page count.

Why do you ask for a not-to-exceed authorization?

Because the page count is unknown until we are at the counter looking at the document. Authorizing a maximum dollar amount for county fees in advance lets us complete the purchase in a single trip instead of stopping to call for approval.

What is the difference between a certified copy and a plain copy?

A certified copy bears the recorder’s official seal and certification statement, making it acceptable to courts, lenders, title companies, and government agencies. A plain copy has no official standing. In LA County the difference is $1.00 — if any institution will rely on the document, order it certified.

Can you get copies of documents recorded decades ago?

Yes. LA County official records run back to 1851. Records from 1958 forward are indexed on the second floor at Norwalk and 1851–1957 in the lower level, and anything before 1985 is retrieved from archives rather than same-day at the counter.

What types of documents can you retrieve?

Anything in the recorder’s official records index: grant deeds, quitclaim deeds, interspousal and trust transfer deeds, deeds of trust, reconveyances, mechanics and judgment liens, notices of default, easements, CC&Rs, affidavits of death, and fictitious business name statements.
