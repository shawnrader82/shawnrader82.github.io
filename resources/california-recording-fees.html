    Fee breakdown

    How California Recording Fees Are Calculated

    Every recording fee in California is set by state law, not by county choice. The recorder collects each one for a specific program — from affordable housing (SB2) to fraud prosecution to restrictive covenant research (AB 1466).

    Base Recording Fee

    $12–$15 + $3/page
    Gov. Code § 27361

    Covers recording, indexing, and storing your document. Each county sets its first-page rate within state limits: LA charges $15, Orange $12, and Ventura $14. Every additional page is $3 in all three counties.

    SB2 Building Homes & Jobs Act

    $75 per title
    Gov. Code § 27388.1

    Enacted in 2017 to fund affordable housing. Applies to most documents that transfer or affect title to real property, including deeds. Capped at $225 per transaction (up to 3 fees).

    It does not apply to documents recorded with a sale that is subject to documentary transfer tax, so buyer-financed home purchases are typically exempt.

    The Two Real Estate Fraud Fees — They Are Not the Same

    This is the most common source of quoting errors. Los Angeles County collects two separate fraud-related fees under different statutes, and the instrument decides which one applies.

    Fraud Notification Fee — $7

    Gov. Code §§ 27297.6, 27387.1. Applies to grant deeds and quitclaim deeds. Pays for the notice the recorder mails to the owner of record when a document affecting title is recorded.

    Real Estate Fraud Fee — $10

    Gov. Code § 27388. The District Attorney fee for fraud investigation and prosecution. Applies to other recordings, including transfer-on-death deeds, involuntary liens, and quitclaim deeds not subject to transfer tax. LA raised it from $5 to $10 on June 1, 2026.

    A quitclaim deed that is exempt from documentary transfer tax can carry both fees. Because the combination depends on how the instrument is classified at intake, confirm the total at the counter before committing to a fixed quote.

    AB 1466 Restrictive Covenant Fee

    $2 per document
    Gov. Code § 27388.2

    Enacted in 2021. Funds county programs that find and redact unlawful restrictive covenants (racial, ethnic, or religious restrictions in historical deeds). Applies to almost every recordable document.

    Certified Copies

    Per page
    Gov. Code § 27366

    Needed for courts, insurers, and lenders. Charged per page, not per document.

    • LA: $6 first page, $3 each additional (plain copy $5 + $3)
    • Orange: $1 per page + $1 to certify
    • Ventura: $2 first page, $1 each additional, + $1 to certify

    A three-page certified grant deed costs $12 in LA, $4 in Orange, and $5 in Ventura. Recorders sell the complete document only, never a single page. LA charges a non-refundable $0.50 per name, per year index search ($1 minimum); Orange and Ventura staff do not search for the public, so bring the document number or book and page.     What Isn't Included in Recording Fees

    • Documentary transfer tax — typically $1.10 per $1,000 of consideration on property sales.
    • Preliminary Change of Ownership Report (PCOR) — free, but required with most deeds.
    • Notary fees — $15 per signature under Gov. Code § 8211, plus travel and handling for a mobile Notary visit.

    Common questions

    California Recording Fees FAQ

    Why is my recording bill higher than the base fee?

    Because a single recording includes several fees set by state law. A 3-page grant deed in LA County breaks down as $15 first page + $6 additional pages + $75 SB2 + $7 fraud notification fee + $2 AB 1466 = $105. That covers county fees only — a mobile document recording service adds a trip and handling fee on top.

    How much does it cost to record a grant deed in Los Angeles County?

    For a 3-page grant deed that is not part of a sale, LA County fees total $105: $15 first page, $6 for two more pages, $75 SB2, $7 Real Estate Fraud Notification Fee, and $2 AB 1466. Use the calculator above to price your exact page count and document type.

    What is the SB2 fee?

    SB2, the Building Homes and Jobs Act (Gov. Code § 27388.1), is a $75 fee per title on most documents that transfer or affect title to real property. It is capped at $225 per transaction and does not apply to documents recorded with a sale that is subject to documentary transfer tax.

    What is the difference between the two real estate fraud fees?

    The $7 Real Estate Fraud Notification Fee (Gov. Code §§ 27297.6, 27387.1) applies to grant and quitclaim deeds and pays for the notice mailed to the owner of record. The $10 Real Estate Fraud Fee (Gov. Code § 27388) funds District Attorney fraud prosecution and applies to other recordings, such as transfer-on-death deeds and involuntary liens. A quitclaim deed exempt from transfer tax can carry both.

    How much is a certified copy of a recorded deed?

    Copies are priced per page. A three-page certified grant deed costs $12 in Los Angeles County, $4 in Orange County, and $5 in Ventura County. LA also charges a non-refundable $0.50 per name, per year index search fee ($1 minimum) if you do not have the document number.

    Are recording fees the same for e-recording?

    Yes. The same county fee schedule applies whether a document is recorded at the counter, by mail, or electronically through an approved submitter.

    What if my document is missing a required disclosure or exhibit?

    The recorder rejects the document and mails it back, which costs 5–10 business days plus return postage. Common rejection reasons are a missing legal description, a missing PCOR on a deed, an incorrect APN, or unpaid transfer tax. A pre-filing review catches these before submission.

    Do recording fees include transfer tax or notary fees?

    No. Recording fees do not include documentary transfer tax (typically $1.10 per $1,000 of consideration on property sales), the PCOR form (free but required), or notary fees. Notarization is $15 per signature under Gov. Code § 8211, plus travel for a mobile Notary visit.
